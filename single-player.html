Is there single-player? No — here's exactly what that means

There is no campaign, no story mode, and no bot matches. Hell Let Loose: Vietnam is a 50v50 multiplayer game, full stop. Before you spend $39.99 hoping otherwise, here's what the evidence says solo players actually get.

The proof, not just our word

  • The official Steam page lists exactly one play feature: Online PvP (plus cross-platform multiplayer). Genre tags: Massively Multiplayer. Every mode described — Warfare, Offensive — is "epic 50 vs 50 multiplayer battles".
  • A broadband connection is listed under minimum requirements — the game assumes you're online.
  • Community documentation is blunt: "multiplayer-only. There is no campaign, no bot matches, and no offline mode" (community FAQ).
  • Nothing in either official Dev Q&A hints at a co-op or PvE mode arriving later.

What you CAN do without other humans

The store page promises a "revamped tutorial and player onboarding" — a first for a series that used to throw you into the meat grinder cold. This is real, tested content, not marketing: beta players reported spending "a good hour in the tutorial", running "the training stuff" before matches, and there's footage of the helicopter tutorial from the PS5 playtest. So yes: you can learn movement, weapons and helicopter flying solo. What we can't confirm pre-launch is whether tutorials load with your internet unplugged — the game is built online-first, so treat "solo" as "alone on your side of the screen", not offline. We test that on day one.

Should a pure single-player fan buy it?

Honestly: no. Tutorials are maybe your first evening; everything after is 99 other live humans. If bots or a campaign are your condition, skip it — no roadmap suggests they're coming. The gray zone is the mic-shy player: you can absolutely play without ever speaking (listening is what matters), and the tutorial ramp makes that easier than the original HLL. If that's you, weigh the standard vs Deluxe question and check how crossplay servers work before launch day. And remember the safety net: Steam refunds anything under 2 hours played within 14 days — enough to run the tutorial and one real match.
